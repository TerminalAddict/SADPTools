01 / Find & connect

SADP & ONVIF discovery

Find compatible cameras and recorders in one live inventory.

  • Choose an adapter or all suitable adapters; use one-off or automatic scans.
  • Search and sort devices, distinguish inactive equipment and export CSV or JSON.
  • Keep results visible across scans, with progress, cancellation and diagnostic logs.

Multicast discovery normally stays within the local broadcast domain. SADP can find compatible devices with addresses outside the host subnet.

04 / Live View & imaging

Live View in its own window

Open a camera and start watching without a second play-button step.

  • Resolve supported Hikvision or ONVIF streams and choose main or sub stream.
  • Keep viewing while you move through the site and diagnostic tools.
  • Pause, stop or use fullscreen; retain visible playback and connection status.

Playback uses RTSP over TCP. Try H.264 first on Apple Mac; H.265/HEVC depends on the OS and decoder. Stream and credential support also depend on the camera.

Stream information & connection tests

Understand what the video connection is doing.

  • Inspect available codec, resolution, timing and playback information.
  • Run bounded connection tests and compare main and sub streams.
  • Review the connection timeline and save diagnostic evidence to the site.

Unavailable metrics stay unknown. Encoded frame rate and displayed frame rate are different measurements.

05 / Network & diagnostics

Connection troubleshooter

Check the actual route to the selected device.

  • Inspect source, route, gateway and neighbour information on Windows, Linux and Apple Mac, then run bounded TCP/HTTP/HTTPS checks.
  • Optionally include ICMP and approved ONVIF or RTSP metadata reads.
  • Compare with a trusted SSH probe and copy a safe diagnostic summary.

Routed cameras do not have to share your subnet. Cached neighbour entries are not proof of a live device; a timeout or blocked ping alone does not establish failure.
